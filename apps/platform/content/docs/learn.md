Jobs keep asking for tools that are newer than your last project. **Learn** collects them, so before a first or second round you know exactly what to study — and gives you notes to study from.

## How tools get on the list

When you **approve jobs** in an [automation](/docs/automations), every skill those jobs ask for that isn't on your profile is added to Learn, with the jobs that asked for it. The approval step names them: *New to learn: CrewAI, MCP, Qdrant — see Learn.*

You can also add a tool by hand on the Learn page.

Offerloop recognises a wide range of tools in job descriptions, including the fast-moving AI stack: LangChain, LangGraph, LlamaIndex, CrewAI, AutoGen, DSPy, MCP, vector databases (Pinecone, Weaviate, Qdrant, Milvus, pgvector, FAISS, Chroma), Langfuse, LangSmith, MLflow, Bedrock, Vertex AI, vLLM, Ollama, fine-tuning (LoRA, PEFT) and more.

> Want jobs like these to make the cut in the first place? In the **Find new jobs** step, set **New tools in the JD** to **Don't count — add to Learn**. See [role first](/docs/jobs#role-first-when-new-tools-shouldn-t-count-against-you).

## Study notes

Open a tool and click **Write study notes**. Using your AI settings from [Resume Studio](/docs/resume-studio#set-up-your-ai-key), Offerloop writes:

- **What it is** and why companies use it;
- **From what you know** — how it maps onto tools on your profile (e.g. *"a Crew is like a LangGraph graph whose nodes are agents"*);
- **How it fits together** — a diagram of its main pieces;
- **Key concepts** to understand and talk about;
- **Quick start** — a short, real code example;
- **Likely interview questions**, each with what a strong answer covers;
- **Get interview-ready** — a short plan, in hours or days.

Notes are saved to your account. **Rewrite** gets a fresh version. They're written by AI: check details against the official docs (there's a link on each page).

## Track your progress

Mark each tool **Learning** or **I know it**. Tools you know move out of the way. Once you really know one, add it to your **Career Profile** skills — then jobs asking for it score higher.
